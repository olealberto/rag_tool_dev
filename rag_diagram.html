How grant text becomes searchable vectors in the RAG pipeline

01
📄
RAW GRANT TEXT INPUT
Grant abstracts, aims, and section chunks ingested from 42 PDF files. Chunks are section-prefixed (e.g. [AIMS], [METHODS]) to preserve document structure. Total corpus: 3,986 chunks across 1,889 unique grant IDs.

02
✂️
WORDPIECE TOKENIZATION
Text is split into subword tokens using BERT's WordPiece vocabulary (30,522 tokens). Rare or complex biomedical terms are decomposed into known subwords, preserving semantic meaning while handling out-of-vocabulary words.

03

PubMedBERT TRANSFORMER ENCODER
12 stacked transformer layers, each with 12 attention heads (768 hidden dimensions). Pre-trained exclusively on PubMed abstracts — no general web text. Each layer refines contextual token representations through self-attention.

04

MEAN POOLING → SENTENCE VECTOR
All token embeddings are averaged (mean pooling) into a single 768-dimensional sentence vector. This collapses the variable-length token sequence into a fixed-size representation of the entire chunk's meaning.

05
🗄️
WEAVIATE HNSW VECTOR INDEX
All 3,986 chunk vectors are stored in Weaviate using HNSW (Hierarchical Navigable Small World) indexing. At query time, approximate nearest-neighbor search retrieves the top-k most semantically similar chunks in milliseconds via cosine similarity.
cosine similarity scores
06
⚡
HYBRID SEARCH FUSION (optimal α = 0.25)
BM25 KEYWORD (75%)
Term frequency × inverse document frequency over section-prefixed text. Exact NIH grant ID matching, funding mechanism codes, clinical terminology. Strong on precise biomedical vocabulary.
VECTOR SEMANTIC (25%)
PubMedBERT cosine similarity in 768-dim space. Captures paraphrasing, synonymy, and cross-topic relationships. Generalizes beyond exact term matches.
score = (1 − α) × BM25_norm + α × vector_sim  |  α = 0.25
